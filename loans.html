How it works

    
In EU5 a loan is interest only: each month you pay amount × rate ÷ 12, and the whole
      amount falls due at the end of the term. If you can't pay it then, the loan is extended and you lose 3 points of
      creditworthiness. A government bond never falls due: you pay its interest every month until you redeem it.
      So a loan only pays for itself if the project returns its interest and enough to put the principal aside by the
      end of the term; a bond only has to beat its interest.

    

      
Why does so little of a building's profit reach the treasury?
        
A building's profit is wealth in its location, not gold in your treasury. Wealth outside your control goes straight
          to the estates. The rest is tax base, split between the estates by their share of the location's wealth, and each pays
          you its tax rate. The page rebuilds your tax income from the save this way to within about a percent (the tiles show
          both), so a profit of 1 gold a month in a 90%-control city whose estates pay about 30% is worth about 0.27 a month to
          the treasury. The rest still matters — estates grow richer and more content — but it doesn't repay a loan.

      
Where do the costs come from?
        
A building costs its base price — 50 gold for one unlocked in the Age of Traditions, doubling with each age,
          more for expensive ones — times your own cost factor, read from what your constructions in progress
          actually cost in the save (your nation's if it is building anything, otherwise the world's). An RGO expansion costs
          100 × (0.5 + 0.25 × the good's price) times the same kind of factor.

      
How is a new building's income estimated?
        
From the same building elsewhere in the same market: all its profit there divided by all its levels, idle ones
          counting as zero. If any of them is laying off workers, the market can't use more and the page won't suggest it. It also
          needs enough unemployed pops of the building's type in the location, and room under its level cap (development,
          population, rank and market access, as the game's script values have them). The loan plan never adds more levels of a
          building to a market than it already has there, so prices have a chance to hold.

      
What about an RGO?
        
An RGO expansion adds one worker slot. It's valued at what each RGO worker of that good makes in the market, and the
          game turns RGO output into somewhat less location wealth than building profit (the page fits both from your save).
          Unemployed peasants promote into new slots, so they count as available workers.

      
What isn't counted?
        
Building upkeep, price changes as new supply arrives, and everything a building does besides making goods: estate
          satisfaction, development, prosperity, levies. Interest rates move with creditworthiness; the page starts from the
          lowest rate you pay now (or the world's going rate) and you can change it.
